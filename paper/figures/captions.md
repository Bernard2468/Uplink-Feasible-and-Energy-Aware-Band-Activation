Fig. 5. Held-out energy saving and service reliability of the proposed MPC controller. (a), (b) Saving relative to all-on operation, 1 − E/E_aon, with energy pooled over the 25 test squares and 100 radio realizations, under S0 (published hardware model) and S1, S2 (assumed added equipment overhead). Error bars are 95% pigeonhole bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples; squares and realizations resampled independently). (c), (d) Avoidable violation share: the share of 10-minute intervals in which more than 5% of all users are in DL or UL outage although some activation pattern would have met the target, plus the seconds of wake-up gaps in which the previous pattern fails. The dashed line is the 1% avoidable-violation budget. The shaded band in (d) marks growth levels where the unavoidable share (no pattern meets the target) exceeds 1% (Fig. 7). Scenarios with identical decisions share one line (10/90: S1 = S2; 26/74: S0 = S1 = S2). All 42 primary cells meet the budget (maximum 0.95%). In the registered H1 cells (g = 1, 2) the saving is 4.76–10.38% under S0 and 18.92–42.20% under S2.

Fig. 8. Controller comparison, optimality gap and hardware robustness (test week). (a) Frozen operating points of the online controllers and two perfect-foresight references at 26/74, g = 2, S2: saving relative to all-on against avoidable violation share (axis broken between 1.25% and 1.5%); points right of the dashed line fail the budget.

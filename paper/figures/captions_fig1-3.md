## fig1_network_layout_w1

Fig. 1. The 19-site network at an inter-site distance (ISD) of 200 m in the Munich scene of Sionna RT. An ITU-R M.2412 hexagonal lattice (rings 0–2, no wrap-around) is anchored at the scene's base station (site 0, 27 m). Every other lattice point is moved to the nearest roof within 0.2·ISD (17 rooftop sites) or, where there is none, given a ground mast (1 site); these antennas are at 25 m. The map is the scene geometry on the 4 m sample grid within 600 m of site 0: buildings (grey), open outdoor points (white), and grid points that are neither (light tint; outside the sampled area or excluded by the sampling rules). Each site has three co-sited sectors with boresights at 30°, 150°, 270° (TR 38.901), numbered 1–3 as the bits of an activation pattern (labels at site 0); each sector has a 3.5 GHz and a 7.2 GHz cell. Thin lines are the nominal hexagonal cells. Users are scored in site 0's cell (bold; 935 outdoor points and 5,756 indoor samples), with their line-of-sight state, facade and indoor depth toward every site taken from the scene; path loss is TR 38.901 UMa.

## fig2_interference_feasibility_w2

Fig. 2. Directional interference and the resulting UL/DL feasibility at 7.2 GHz (ISD 200 m; case A: 8×16 arrays at both carriers, 20 MHz; all cells on at full load; users in site 0's cell, 935 outdoor points and 5,756 indoor samples, M.2412 mix of 20% in-car and 80% indoor users; 100 realizations). Bars and markers are the recorded 1st, 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles of the DL SINR and the full-band UL SINR under four interference models: none (19-site association, noise only); averaged over beam directions (E1c.2); directional, with each interfering beam steered to its own scheduled user and the angular spread calibrated to the measured beamforming deficit (primary, E1c.3); and the pure array pattern. Only these four percentiles were recorded; no distribution is drawn between them. Dashed lines: the F2 service thresholds, 12.92 dB DL and 8.66 dB UL (F2: the M.2410 Dense Urban 5th-percentile user spectral efficiency with 10 users per cell). Right column: the share of user positions meeting F2 (Monte-Carlo standard error at most 0.0003). The DL 1st percentile is 14.9, 9.2 and 5.1 dB under the averaged, directional and pure-pattern models (medians 25.9, 25.4 and 27.5 dB), so the DL share falls from 0.995 (averaged) to 0.962 (directional) and 0.919 (pure pattern); the UL share changes by at most 1.0 points.

## fig3_power_energy_w4

**Panel descriptions (under each panel):** (a) Power per cell vs occupancy | (b) Daily site energy, UL/DL 10/90, ETSI day profile

Fig. 3. Base-station power and hardware-overhead sensitivity. (a) Power per cell against resource occupancy L from the source-reproduced models, at the recorded points (markers) joined by straight segments; the 3.5 GHz model is affine in L, and the 7.2 GHz model departs from the segments by less than 1 mW. Shown are the measurement-anchored 3.5 GHz 64T64R model with its idle modes (µDTX and AFE idle; primary; 712.2 W at no load) and without them (891.8 W), and the published FR3 model at 7.2 GHz (50 dBm, SiGe power amplifiers, M_RF = 256; 171.6 W at no load) with equal DL and UL occupancy (the UL term adds at most 11.0 W). S1 and S2 add an assumed equipment overhead in every in-frame (not sleeping) state, and the sleep powers scale with it: 172.0 W (measured components the FR3 model omits) and 540.6 W (the no-load power raised to the 3.5 GHz layer's 712.2 W), so the S2 line stays within 8.6 W of the 3.5 GHz idle-mode line. S1 and S2 are stress tests, not measured FR3 values. Deep sleep (Cat 1) draws 2.9, 6.0, 12.7 W under S0, S1, S2. (b) Daily energy per site (three sectors, both layers) at UL/DL 10/90 over the ETSI ES 202 706-1 day: no traffic 00–06 h, medium level (g·0.635) 06–16 h and busy level (g) 16–24 h, g = 0.5–4. Open markers fail the all-user criterion in some level of the day; dotted lines mark the limits g = 1.45 (3.5 GHz only) and g = 3.72 (always on). The 95% intervals over the 100 radio realizations are at most ±0.16 kWh. Under S0, always-on uses 8.6% more energy than 3.5 GHz only at g = 1, less at g = 1.75–3.25, where 3.5 GHz only already fails, and more again from g = 3.5, where the 3.5-GHz-only energy stays at 82.83 kWh. S1 and S2 add 12.39 and 38.92 kWh per day. The 3.5-GHz-only curve (7.2 GHz cells in deep sleep all day) is drawn for S0; under S1 and S2 it is higher by at most 0.23 and 0.71 kWh (deep-sleep power).
